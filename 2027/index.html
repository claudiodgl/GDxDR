About

Graph drawing and dimensionality reduction share many methodological, theoretical and practical challenges: scalability, ambiguity, and the reliability of what a layout appears to show. Yet the two communities often develop in parallel, with limited exchange of techniques, assumptions and evaluation practices.

The workshop is a space for open, critical and constructive discussion where the fields meet. Core themes are common evaluation pitfalls and their alternatives, how domain experts actually use these methods, and reliability questions such as comparing real and synthetic user data.

The first GDxDR at EuroVis 2026 in Nottingham drew a full room and contributions from well-known researchers on both sides. The 2027 edition takes place at EuroVis 2027 in Stuttgart, Germany, from 31 May to 4 June 2027, hosted by the University of Stuttgart and Stuttgart Technical University of Applied Sciences. No prior expertise in GD or DR is needed to attend, and participation is not limited to authors.

Outcome 1

A community statement

A shared position on evaluation and methodology: the pitfalls both fields hit and what better practice looks like.

Outcome 2

A roadmap

How each field can benefit from the other, with open challenges and research directions mapped by participants.

Outcome 3

New collaborations

Connections across GD and DR groups that lead to joint projects, co-authored papers and future editions.

Topics

We welcome work on any connection between the two fields, including:

Shared methods and theory

How principles from graph theory inform DR and vice versa: stress, neighbourhoods, embeddings, layouts.

Evaluation pitfalls and alternatives

Quality metrics that are correlated, conflicting or insufficient alone, and better evaluation strategies.

Interpretability and trust

Stability, trustworthiness and embedding quality, and how people read and trust the result.

Reliability of user data

Comparing real and synthetic user data in studies and benchmarks.

Scalability and ambiguity

Methods and metrics that hold up for large networks and high-dimensional data.

Domain-expert usage scenarios

Case studies and applications where one field successfully informed the other.

Network visualization

Multivariate and multilayer networks, node types, flow and dynamics where they meet embedding techniques.

Critiques of current practice

Assumptions both communities make about readability and faithfulness.

Call for papers

We invite researchers, practitioners and educators interested in the connections between GD and DR, from established scholars to early-career researchers. We particularly encourage work that shows cross-domain relevance, proposes new methodological approaches, or reports on concrete application scenarios.

Short paper4pages incl. references

Case studies, usage scenarios, comparative analyses or methodological contributions that highlight links between GD and DR. Empirical studies, critiques of current practice, new evaluation strategies, and reports where one field successfully informed the other are all welcome.

Review2+reviewers per paper

At least one reviewer is a member of the program committee. Papers are judged on clarity, relevance and potential to stimulate discussion. Novel insights and critical reflection count as much as empirical rigour.

  • Accepted papers are published in the Eurographics Digital Library.
  • Accepted papers are presented as lightning talks of 5–10 minutes, leaving time for discussion.
